================================================================================
  PHILOLOGICAL COMMENTARY
================================================================================

1. The shared core is Aristotle's rule of predication from
   Categories 1b10: "ὅσα κατά τινος λέγεται, ταῦτα καὶ
   κατὰ τοῦ ὑποκειμένου ῥηθήσεται."

2. What makes this match significant is NOT the shared
   Aristotelian quote — that would be a commonplace.
   Rather, the significance lies in what BOTH authors do
   with the same quote:

   - Psellos embeds it in an 11th-century anti-Eunomian
     polemic: Eunomius of Cyzicus (4th c., Anomoean
     theologian) allegedly used this rule fallaciously
     to deny the consubstantiality of the Son.

   - Eustratius, writing a formal commentary on the
     Posterior Analytics a century later, reproduces not
     just the Aristotelian lemma but the PSELLIAN
     syntactic framework — the qualifying clause
     "οὐ γὰρ ἁπλῶς... ἀλλὰ δῆλον" (not simply...
     but it is clear) with which Psellos qualified the
     predication rule.

3. Because neither Proclus nor Psellos appears in the
   indices nominum of the standard editions of Eustratius's
   commentaries, this match represents a genuine, hitherto
   unmapped instance of covert text reuse.
